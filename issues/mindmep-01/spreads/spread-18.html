Two markets, one thread

Across Singapore and Malaysia, Shan leads with a consistent thread: resilience. “Hire for attitude and potential,” he says. “Then invest. Train. Align on goals. And stay present.”

He knows each market comes with its own rhythm. Regulations differ. Work cultures clash. But adaptability isn’t a skill you teach; it is something you practise. And Shan has been practising it for years.

The subcontractor model that Mint operates on isn’t always an easy one, but for Shan the key is clear communication, constant coordination, and never assuming something is under control until you have verified it yourself.

“The ones who impress me most aren’t just technically strong. They’re proactive. They solve problems before I even know they’re problems.”

Looking back, he is proud of what Mint has become. But he doesn’t romanticise it. He knows what it took to get here.

And if there is one thing he hopes the next generation of engineers understands, it is this: “Be curious. Stay hands-on. Take ownership. And be ready to fall down a few times. That’s where the real learning happens.”

Because in this industry, the path to leadership doesn’t always look like a ladder.

Sometimes it looks like scaffolding: messy, temporary, but strong enough to carry you if you are willing to climb.
