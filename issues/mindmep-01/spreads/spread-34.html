ONGOING — CHINA SQUARE
Project Sherlock

Each shophouse carries a distinct story. Timber floors, ornate façades, and narrow airwells leave very little room for modern services. Mint’s job is to thread infrastructure through these layers without disturbing their character.

The first hurdle was asbestos, identified early and removed by specialists before other works began. The decision added time upfront but created a safe and predictable site for everyone that followed.

Low ceilings led to a decentralised ACMV scheme with slim fan coils above door heads and discreet bulkheads. Ground-floor F&B extracts were manifolded at the rear to keep façades clean and roof terminations to a minimum.

Upgrades that stay invisible

Plumbing added grease management and compact booster sets in under-stair niches. A consolidated substation now feeds metered wayleaves to each unit.

Where chasing would harm brickwork, surface containment is concealed behind joinery or within new partitions.

Precision, sequencing and sensitivity

Working alongside main contractor partners, Mint planned trade sequencing with care. When a new escalator opening was cut between two shophouses, the team used that window to install vertical ducting and cabling before slabs were closed.

In a live CBD environment, quieter compact piling rigs reduced vibration and protected fragile foundations. Project Sherlock is still unfolding, yet the lessons are clear: progress in conservation comes from precision and restraint.

As each shophouse reopens, façades read as they always have while modern systems remain hidden. Visitors will feel the history rather than the engineering, which is exactly the outcome Mint aims for.

Together, BeyondX and Project Sherlock show that conservation engineering is craft and care in equal measure. Progress need not erase history.
